# **What is Free Company and Meta Ruleset?** {#what-is-free-company-and-meta-ruleset?}

The Meta Ruleset is a **cooperative** combined arms tabletop roleplaying wargame built as a simple set of rules for players needing structure to run large scale tactical combat. From medieval castle sieges and modern tank battles to fighting with massive warships in space, the Meta Ruleset works for any number of players needed for any size group.  “**Free Compan**y” is the setting ARMCO uses for its ongoing community setting. This setting is a *“Cold Science-Fiction”* universe where players assume the role of commanders for different unique **companies of mercenaries** in the year 3025 traveling the galaxy trying to eke out an existence in an uncaring universe. This setting can be played on its own or as a companion to your favorite RPG\! Leading your characters forces to victory\!

# **Core Mechanics**  {#core-mechanics}

## Components: What you need to play\! {#components:-what-you-need-to-play!}

**Miniatures / Icons**: This is a miniatures focused wargame after all\! Digital or physical doesn't matter. Free Company is a mini agnostic ruleset meaning not only can you play with any minis you already have but you can play at whatever scale your space can support. Be it 8mm tiny minis or standard 28mm heroic scale. Part of the fun is finding minis that fit your needs\!   
**The Unit Stats**: This booklet comes with all the basic unit statistics you’ll need to get started. These stats tell you all about each unit, its strengths and weaknesses.   
**Some Dice**: We recommend a bunch of D6s and D4s. If you don't have any D4s, D6s can be used instead just reroll any dice that go over the number range you need.   
**Intention Indicators**: Color-coded tokens are available in this booklet to print and cut out. These tokens allow players to easily keep track of order types, where a player would like to move, and what direction they will face on the battlefield during planning and combat.   
**Hit Markers:** Some small object to mark when a vehicle has taken damage. Included in this booklet are printable hit markers, but really anything will work.  We’ve used everything from small candies to small washers or nuts.    
**Measuring Tool:** Depending on the size of your table, you’ll want some kind of measuring tool to move your units around the battlefield and check weapon ranges. Measuring tapes or a ruler works great. We measure from the center of the base / token. 

## **Understanding Unit Stats** {#understanding-unit-stats}

Each unit is defined by its 4 Core Statistics: how much damage can a unit take before it's destroyed **(Force Strength** or **Hits**), how fast a unit is or how much said unit can accomplish per round (**Speed**),  how resistant is the unit to taking damage (**Armor**), and finally its weapon’s capabilities (**Range and Damage**).

### **Force Strength (FS)**  {#force-strength-(fs)}

Force Strength (FS)  is a mix of health and combat capability for the unit **personnel**. 1FS generally means 1 individual in a group. A squad of 6 Infantry are 6 Force Strength. When FS reaches zero the unit has been destroyed. Unless otherwise stated, all Force Strength Attacks use a D6 and max their damage at the number of FS in the unit. For example: a Force Strength 3 Unit rolls a D6 but can only do up to a maximum of 3 Damage. 

### **Hits**    {#hits}

The health measurement of a vehicle unit. Hits represent the number of successful enemy attacks the vehicle can sustain before it is destroyed. If a vehicle’s Hits reach zero then that vehicle is destroyed. Taking a Hit requires incoming attack damage to be higher than the vehicle's armor or defense. Some units have multiple weapons firing per round. Each individual weapon must overcome armor and defense to inflict damage or a hit

### **Speed**   {#speed}

A measurement of how far a unit can move per round and a resource measuring how much a unit can accomplish in a round when doing **Actions**. Speed represents how far a unit can move each round where the distance could be (X) inches on a table, number of Hex grids out, or even squares depending on the battlemap played on. (Where X \= the same measurement as weapon range.) Unique to this ruleset: there are no hard limits on the number of actions a unit can accomplish if it has the **Speed** to do so. Some equipment can lower the speed cost of some actions.

####  **Actions** {#actions}

Speed is a **resource** to be spent doing **standard actions**. **Standard Actions** cost **0.5 speed per action** or attempt. They are the majority of actions taken. Landing, taking off, getting out of a vehicle, getting into a vehicle, Special Objective interactions,  Loading another unit into cargo as the vehicle unit, doing repairs, setting up a heavy weapons system as infantry. If it’s an action you want to accomplish, then it uses speed.   
(with the exception of the Heavy Air Transport, which uses 0.5 speed per cargo slot).

**Primary Actions** are actions that can only be taken once per round and use up any attack that unit would have done if it was able. If you do an action that's labeled **“Primary Action**” your unit can not attack. This action does not use speed. 

### **Armor**  {#armor}

Armor determines how resistant to damage a vehicle is. If a vehicle is attacked the damage of said attack has the armor amount subtracted. If the armor is equal to or greater than the incoming damage then the attack has bounced off the armor and no hit is taken. 

Armor can be overcome by **Armor Piercing (AP)** attacks. If an attack has Armor piercing, subtract the amount of armor piercing from the total armor before determining damage.  

*Example: Your main battle tank is attacked by a 6FS infantry squad. The squad rolls a 3 for damage. Your armor is 3\. Meaning the tank has bounced any attacks and no damage is taken.* 

*Example: Your main battle tank is attacked by a 6FS infantry squad with an Anti-Tank weapon giving them AP+1. The enemy squad rolls a 3 for damage. Your armor is 3 but with the enemy AP of 1, that makes your armor effectively a 2\. 3 Damage beats your now 2 armor. Meaning your tank takes 1 Hit.* 

##### **Defense** {#defense}

Works like armor but is unaffected by AP. Defense is gained in a few ways but normally based upon actions on the battlefield such as infantry using the “Dig In” Action or some vehicle’s **Evade** order type.

### **Range**  {#range}

The distance your unit’s weapon can attack an enemy.  1 Range means your unit can fire at 1 out from the unit's current position. The range could be measured in (X) inches on a table, Number of Hex grids out or even squares depending on the battlemap played on. This amount is measured the same as the distance a unit can move using speed. 

### **Damage** {#damage}

Damage is simply the amount of damage your attack can do. For vehicles this is represented by a type of dice based on the weapon system or systems they have equipped. For example a Heavy Machine Gun on a light vehicle rolls a D4,.  Personnel (anyone not in a vehicle) **always roll a D6** and limit damage possible by how much FS they currently have remaining. Some units have multiple weapons firing per round. Each individual weapon must overcome armor and defense to inflict damage or a hit.

### **Req Value**   {#req-value}

**Requisition Value** is the cost or value of a unit. Used for buying and customizing your army.  

### **Orbital: Customization Slots** {#orbital:-customization-slots}

The number of orbital equipment a warship can have. These slots are used for building out warships and defining their capability.

### **Orbital: Atmo-Fuel**  {#orbital:-atmo-fuel}

The number of rounds an orbital can stay in low orbit, fly at low altitude providing direct support on a tactical map. Running out of Atmo-fuel forces the orbital back into high orbit. Orbitals burn 1 fuel for every round they are flying over a planetary tactical map. Landed orbitals do not burn atmo-fuel while landed. 

# Playing the Game {#playing-the-game}

## Player Intentions Phase {#player-intentions-phase}

One unique mechanic of the Meta Ruleset is the fact that it's designed from the ground up to be cooperative. Players take their turns simultaneously. Talking through their plan and working together. Players choose what **actions** they want a unit to do and how they want each unit to behave by picking an order type. There is no limit to the number of actions a unit can take as long as they have the speed available, players then place the units order type token where they wish their unit to end up at the end of the turn. Think of this like giving your unit an order on the battlefield. The enemy will do the same and where those units come into range of each other is where a battle takes place. While we plan, we never know for sure what the enemy will do. 

### Standard Order Types {#standard-order-types}

#### **Hold** {#hold}

Unit holds its current position and uses none of its speed to move. They can still commit to actions such as deploying as artillery, setting up equipment or use actions like "Dig In" if available to that unit type. Unit will fire at hostels that come into range. .  

#### **Advance** {#advance}

Move Order.  Engage enemies along a designated route. If the path remains clear the unit will continue on the designated route. If hostile forces block the path. They will be stopped at the block point. Mech’s and Aerospace  units are not stopped by hostile forces.

#### **Rush** (Ground Units Only) {#rush-(ground-units-only)}

Ground units **move** at two times the distance per speed point but take twice as much damage (Hits or FS) if attacked and **can’t attack for the round**. Depending on enemy type, units under rush might become the primary target. The Bugs do like fast food after all.

### Special Order Types {#special-order-types}

These Order Types are only for specific units or units with special equipment.. 

#### **Evasive**  {#evasive}

Defensive movement lowering one's accuracy but increasing one's defense represented by  \-2 to attacks \+3 to Defense. The unit must end its turn at least half its total speed worth of distance away **from its starting point** under Evasive Order Type. Only available to some specific units. See unit description.  

#### **Melee Charge** {#melee-charge}

When a unit has a **melee weapon** it can commit to what's called a melee charge. Move towards an enemy unit with the intention of engaging them at point blank range. This can be a great way of disrupting an enemy unit from acting as well as blurring the lines between friend and foe.   Committing to a charge has a few effects. Firstly it requires your unit to commit to a charge, forgoing any range attacks for this round. The goal is to  **move into base contact** with the enemy, this charge can be done at **1.5 Range for infantry / power armor** from the target.  Giving infantry slightly longer movement for the attack.  Second it forces that enemy to fight the charging unit specifically **if caught by the charge**, if the unit is ranged and has not already attacked they get to fire first with damage applied before the melee chargers attack. 

 The surviving charging units can now attack back without dealing with any **defense** bonus of the target unit. Armor still applies if applicable.  Once charged both units are considered to be in a **Melee Brawl** and no other unit can fire ranged weapons into the brawl due to the risk of harming friendly forces. 

Some units have used a desperate melee charge as a way to survive an overwhelming ranged attack from the enemy force by getting so close to said force that they can’t take advantage of their overwhelming firepower. 

To support friendly forces in a brawl one must either have a melee weapon and join into the brawl with your own charge or have weapons that are tagged as highly accurate.

#### **Stealth** {#stealth}

A unit with the stealth tag is attempting to move without being spotted.  Only available to some specific units. See unit description as stealth has 2 separate mechanics.  

Once the players have intentions on the battlefield for all their units, it’s the enemies turn to do the same. 

## Enemy Intentions Phase {#enemy-intentions-phase}

Just like the players, now the enemies must define their intentions. Enemy units act based on their backgrounds, if you're running a game with a GM then the **GM should be making decisions from the perspective of that faction**, working off the faction description for inspiration. Insectoids for example prioritising humanoid targets, aggression and overwhelming numbers, pirates love fast flanking maneuvers and the theft of enemy assets. 

## Combat Round  {#combat-round}

Each unit only gets 1 attack roll per round. The dice rolled are representative of the weapon system or (Force Strength) FS of a unit. For example, if a unit has 3 weapon systems in range like a heavy mech, then they will be rolling 3 dice. Units can only fire at units that are in range and in line of sight for direct fire weapons. **Only Ground units can be flanked.** Hitting the enemy from a direct rear shot ignores armor on vehicles and defense from “Dig In” action on infantry. So flank those tanks\! 

As you know, Force Strength or FS is representative of an infantry squad's strength in combat. All infantry roll a D6 and then cap the amount of damage they do at the number of FS they have in a squad. This is for all infantry attacks. 

Player and enemy units' attacks are rolled and damage is applied at the same time. Players choose the target of each unit under their command while enemy units fire in a specific pattern. If the attacking unit has Armor Penetration (AP), it will always prioritize a vehicle target unless no vehicles are in range. While Infantry will always attack infantry targets and work their way up to armored targets. So, hostile tanks will always target other tanks when in range.  Enemy units will always split their fire between units in range. Meaning if 3 tanks are firing at 3 tanks, the 3 non-player tanks will always fire 1 attack per enemy tank and not stack their attacks on a single target. Use this to your advantage and move units in groups for more survivability.   

It is recommended for larger battles to work from the highest armored unit down. 

# Terrain, Structures and Cover {#terrain,-structures-and-cover}

### Terrain Advantage {#terrain-advantage}

When firing from above the target add a \+1 to your attack roll. Grounds Units Only.

### Flanking Rule {#flanking-rule}

Attacks from behind ignore vehicle armor and infantry defense gained from “**Dig In**” when not inside a structure. When submitting your orders make sure you specify what direction your units are facing. Air Assets gain **no benefit** from flanking attacks.

### Structures / Cover and Forests {#structures-/-cover-and-forests}

Moving into a building as infantry or from room to room of a structure (if it has multiple rooms) costs **0.25 speed**. This includes the insides of structures such as Starships or Space Station maps. Infantry gain \+1 Armor while inside buildings or woods / forested areas if attacked from the outside of said structure. 

Infantry gain cover if behind designated cover objects such as the Engineers sandbag walls or other terrain features noted by the players and GM. Normal rule at the table, if it’s solid and can stop a bullet its cover. Multiple sources of cover **do not stack**. Cover is designated between 2 targets. Functional Vehicles are not included.  Cover also blocks Line of Sight for any unit beyond but not “in” said cover. Example being infantry in a first can be fired at, infantry behind a first can’t be seen. So can’t be fired at. Cover is denoted on ARMCO maps as an orange highlight.  

# Line of Sight {#line-of-sight}

Line of Sight is used to determine if a weapon can fire at a target. Can a unit see the unit they want to fire at? Is something blocking their view like Terrain, Forest, or structures? Then they can’t fire at the target. Most artillery units can fire over obstacles. This is called “indirect fire” If your unit or unit upgrade says indirect fire, then they can fire at targets that are beyond direct line of sight but in range and spotted by another friendly unit. All range measurements are made from the **center of the unit's base**.

# Unit TAGS {#unit-tags}

**Sub-System:** This vehicle has subsystems that can be damaged.  If an attack against this unit rolls a 5 or 6 damage, that unit suffers a Subsystem Malfunction. On a 5, the **weapon systems** stop working until repaired. On a 6, the vehicle becomes immobile until repaired.   
**Infantry Stealth**: This is both an order type and a tag. A unit under the Stealth order type is not automatically revealed to enemy forces when entering Line of Sight. When moving through an enemy unit's line of sight, the number of enemy units are counted. The player unit rolls a D6, and iIf they meet or beat the number of units they are stealthing past then they go unnoticed. Attacking, or committing to any interactions that could reveal this unit, does reveal the unit. See equipment for more details on gear that retains “stealth”.  
**Vehicle Stealth:** Spotted at ½ the LOS distance. Rounded up.   
**Rapid Fire:** This is a weapon system that fires quickly. It's very good at spreading damage, usually to large groups of targets. Double Damage against “horde” tagged enemies.   
**Horde:** A cluster enemy type that operates in large groups.  
**Atmo Flight:** Aircraft that require wings to fly, do so at the highest speeds of any unit on the battlefield. Due to those speeds they take skill and precision to fly.  In atmosphere these units require a dedicated runway to land.  This unit can only **either land or take off once per round** but can’t do both.  
**Aerospace Interceptor:** If a unit with this tag attacks an Aerospace unit, it forces the targeted aerospace unit to only attack the Interceptor. If the hostile can’t then it can’t attack during its turn.    
**Aerospace:** The vehicle is capable of Returning to Orbit / descending from orbit takes ½ the units total speed and it can’t return to orbit, or planetside on the same round.   
**Ponderous:** Large vehicle. Always moves in a ponderus fashion in the most direct line to the end of the order type destination. Guns only fire from the final destination point. **“This is used for all orbital vessels”.**  
**Primary:** or primary action \- an action that uses no speed but does take up any attack the unit would have had. 

## Space Combat: Orbital’s {#space-combat:-orbital’s}

Space combat is the realm of the Warship. In Free Company we call these “**Orbitals**”. Each one is as unique as the crew running it. Orbitals unlike ground units are operated by multiple units with each getting a **single Primary Action** just like ground units. *But don’t need to worry about moving inside an orbital.* These actions affect how the vessel operates and what it can accomplish per round. See the [**equipment list**](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1LmWlwaU5jzh9fVNXGZ1YwY_4Ey71VgIxE5DMjt4A5do/edit?gid=751370403#gid=751370403) to get a feel for what orbitals can do.  An orbital has 1 Captain. This is the owner of the ship and the pilot, or can designate a pilot. If the captain is defeated in a boarding action then the ship is considered secured. The captain can be found on the bridge. Borders must defeat any defenders to get to the captain. 

Orbital combat works the same as ground combat. Using the same ranges, movement, armor, hits and rules with a few mechanics to be aware of.. 

**Fire Critical**  
When an orbital takes damage it has a decent chance to spark a fire. On any attack that rolls a D4 or greater a fire is started and a random system is either shut down, or degraded. If a fire continues to the end of the next round the vessel takes 1 hit worth of damage per fire. A **Primary Action** by a member of the taskforce  is needed to fight the fire. 

**Captain**  
This is the owner of the orbital. All captains we’re once the basic crew unit. Captains choose the movement location for the ship on a tactical map. The captain while in charge of a vessel is not a dictator, but a member of the crew and should work with the crew to accomplish the collective goal.The captain is always the last to fight aboard an orbital. If a captain dies a ship is considered secured by boarding forces. 

**High and Low Orbit**  
Aerospace assets including orbitals can move between high orbit and low orbit using half their speed and cannot return to low or high orbit again that round.. Low orbit is the standard ground based tactical map battles are fought on, an orbital descending can choose to descend to any point on a tactical map. . This is the equivalent of taking a mini and placing it on the battlefield. All weapons work the same as they would for any standard unit.  A unit moving to high orbit is removed from the battlefield and is outside of standard weapon ranges of ground forces. Being in high orbit does allow orbitals some unique support options such as launching drop pods or scanning for intel.    
This doesn’t mean High Orbit is safe. Some missions will have enemy space forces who can do the same. When this happens a new tactical map is needed\! For a fight in Space\! 

Things to note \-   
Fighters and bombers can only land or take off once per round.   
Orbitals **WILL ALWAYS BE THE PRIMARY TARGET FOR ALL Range 2+** ground forces who can fire at said orbital. Unlike standard units orbitals can and will be focused fired on.    
Orbitals always fly in the most direct path to their destination and weapons are fired from the orbitals ending location.  Orbital weapons facing direction are always from center of base.

* Carry Capacity   
  * **Has 2 Cargo Slots.**   
    * **6FS Infantry as 1 filled Slot.**   
    * **1 Vehicle counts as 2 filled slots.**   
    * **5 small supplies count as 1 Slot.**  
* Destroyed when Hit. Don’t get hit. Lowest target priority.

Can reload other units as an action using up supply. Target unit does not need to use an action.   
**Primary Action: Coordinated Supply Drop:** Work with Heavy Air Transport to set up a Supply Air Drop. Heavy Air Transport can fly over the Logi Trucks location and drop its cargo of supply up to the max carry capacity of the Logi Unit.  HAT must fly over the logi unit to drop supply.

# LARGE GAMES \- Special Rules  {#large-games---special-rules}

## Running With a Large Number of Players  {#running-with-a-large-number-of-players}

As games get bigger, we use a series of special optional rules to help them run smoother. The Meta Ruleset has been run with as many as 375 players in a single game. To accomplish large scale community games use these optional rules\! Games of this size are assumed to be played online and over the course of weeks if not months. With a round taking days or even a full week to complete. Orders are usually submitted over a community space, like Discord, to the GM or sub-GMs known simply as Tac-Coms

## TAC-COM’s & Battlegroups {#tac-com’s-&-battlegroups}

     	Tac-Coms are GM assistants that run small portions of the game for a set number of players. In our testing, 30 or so players is a good number of players for a Tac-Com to manage. Players submit their intentions to the Tac-Coms in charge of managing their 30 players, or Battlegroup, who then compile all Battlegroup intentions onto maps to be sent to the GM. The GM then takes all the Tac-Com maps and adds them together onto the full battlefield, and runs the enemy forces as with the standard rules with a few exceptions. 

1. Players in games of this scale do not get to choose targets for standard attacks, targets are chosen for their unit in battles based on the same criteria “Armor Pen. vs Armor” as the enemy's targets are chosen in a standard game with tanks always targeting other tanks and down the line.   
2. Battlegroups are a way of breaking down players into smaller groups, normally with a Battlegroup goal or specific strategy. For example, a group of light vehicles and light mechs taking advantage of their high speed by being in a Battlegroup together. These Battlegroups are formed by the community and send orders to a single Tac-Com.  
3. Platoon System: a way of combining large numbers of players into a single pre-made platoon that acts like a “mega unit.” Players vote on what action that larger group takes without going into detail of individual orders. This is a completely optional way of playing.

## Strategic Level Campaigns \- Fighting Bigger Fights\!  {#strategic-level-campaigns---fighting-bigger-fights!}

The strategic level is all about the movement and actions of battle groups and task forces on a larger scale. Basically a map that shows the location of each grouping of players. These players move as a single marker from point to point along paths per round, fighting enemies and completing objectives at these key locations as they choose. These paths range from being on a planet along roads between cities and towns or junction's to orbital routes in space between orbital bodies.

The capabilities and limitations of these groups of players are defined by its member unit type and their equipment. A group of light vehicles and light mechs that are all very fast can move around the strategic map quickly but will probably lack the combat capability of the slower heavier ground units. A fleet with lots of orbital drop pods and VTOL bays can help support or deploy ground battlegroups quickly and efficiently. Get creative\! 

  What is the difference between a **Task Force** and a **Battlegroup**? A Task Force is focused on space and most air operations made up of mostly **orbital** and **aerospace** assets with player units aboard acting as crew. A **Battlegroup** is a ground based army operating on the surface of a planetoid. A **Task Force** in Free Company generally carries **specific** battlegroups in their “Caro Bays” and is the primary way our forces travel the galaxy. 

Cargo Bay’s are the **most important strategic level equipment** orbitals can have. They represent the unlimited possibility of what the orbital “could be” carrying. We call this potential “**Large Supply**” As a way to help facilitate smoother gameplay orbital players don’t have to define what's in their Cargo Bays till the time comes to use them. They then spend “**Large Supply**” **as a group** like a resource to activate strategic **level effects** such as setting up of a massive minefield in a region or the extra thruster fuel to move the battlegroup faster for 1 round on the strategic map. Large Supply can also be used to maintain ground HQ level bases.  These of course are all just examples.  The hope is that we have lots of ways orbital Task Forces can use Cargo Bays to help shape the battlefield.

## Orbital Boarding Parties {#orbital-boarding-parties}

The tight corridors of modern warships make boarding one a difficult task in the best of times. These small spaces limit how many and what kind of units can actually participate in a fire fight when boarding. This is the realm of infantry. **2 Units can participate in a firefight per *way of entering*.** Units attempting to repel boarders simply state they are repelling a specific ***entry way*** of borders for the round and roll combat against said boarders as is standard. If no units attempt to **repel boarders** then the boarding party gets to choose what equipment they **shut down** for that round. “Engines, Weapon System, Sensors, Ect” going for the bridge forces a fight with remaining defenders. When all defenders or the bridge falls the vessel is considered secure. 

Notes-   
\-Defenders can use “**Dig In**” using a **Primary Action aboard an orbital designating an entry way they are preparing an ambush.** This gives the defensive effect of dig in effect. But only against attackers from that entryway. Doing anything else removed this effect.   
\-Orbital Scan ”layout”  before boarding allows borders to choose a subsystem target.  
\-Orbital Scanning can be used to get an estimate of force strength.   
\-Two units can attack or defend any one “entrance point”. 

* Boarding Pods \- Fired into a hostile vessel. See Equipment list.   
* External Hatch Breach \- VTOL must fly an infantry unit to an external hatch and land on it. Hatches are Hits 1\. Armor \= Vessel Armor. Engineers automatically succeed on this roll.  
* Orbital Forced Docking \- Hostile ship must not have moved this round or have its engines disabled from the start of the round. Orbital must come along its port or starboard side to dock.   
* Smaller ships will have less defenders and will be easier to capture than larger vessels.


## Cargo Supply by Size  {#cargo-supply-by-size}

**Large** \- Used on the strategic level by orbital task force groups. This can’t be recouped in a campaign, so what we bring with us is what we have access to. We get this from Cargo Bays on orbitals. This represents our War Chest for the current conflict. When this runs dry. We’re done.  See large scale games and the strategic level above.. HQ’s / Airfields / Orbital Taskforces can be marked as Supplied for 2 rounds when a Large Supply is used.   
**Medium** \- Used by engineers to build **fobs (Forward Operating Base)** and keep them online. These are much smaller than an HQ Level Base. Each Medium supplied keeps these smaller bases operational for 1 round. Carried by Heavy Air Transport, Heavy VTOL and upgraded logi trucks. Seen on military installations all over. A fob supplies an unlimited number of small supplies while it’s operational to units who can hold it. Just like an H but can’t supply Medium Supply. Medium Supply is required to keep a Fob Running.  
**Small** \- The standard unit of supply  the majority of players care about. Pulled from any currently supplied base or orbital. Logi and VTOL's run this to units in need. Repair parts to artillery shells and medical supplies. This is what's used. Almost all units use this when needing supply. 

**Supply Flow…**   
**Large Supply** \- Carried by Orbitals. Used to Supply Orbital Taskforces and Ground HQ’s  
\-Using 1 Large Supply in an Orbital Taskforce marks the taskforce as “Supplied” for 2 rounds. When “Supplied” taskforce / HQ has the following effect.   
	Logistical Units can Pull Small / Medium Supply  
	Units can reload / rearm / repair from appropriate bays. (Mechs from Mech Bay, Aerospace from hangers..ect)  
Onboard orbital weapons can be reloaded.   
 

**Medium Supply** \- Carried by select few units to build Fobs, pulled only from supplied **HQ Airfields on the surface, supplied orbitals ..**  
**Small Supply** \- Pulled from Orbitals with a Large Supply, HQ Airfields that are online and Fobs that are online. Online: Currently Supplied with their needed supply. 

## Equipment {#equipment}

Equipment is any upgrade that is attached to a unit without changing what the base unit is. Equipment can change how someone behaves. Such as a Heavy Machine Gun Team for infantry has special rules and will change how your unit looks on the tactical map. Read through equipment carefully. 

## Death and Gear {#death-and-gear}

If any unit is destroyed any gear or refits attached to said unit will be lost forever. Remember this when investing in your units.  Dead is Dead and most campaigns have a very high lethality rate.
